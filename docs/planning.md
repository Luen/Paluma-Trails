# Planning a walk

The planner answers “how far is that, and how long will it take?” from the
mapped junction network. It is a planning aid, not a substitute for a map and
compass, and it only uses tracks the content actually has.

## What it does

Open it from the route icon in the map's app bar. Pick junctions and a shape:

- **One way** — the shortest walk from one junction to another.
- **Out and back** — the same walk there and back; the total is doubled and the
  sheet also shows the distance each way.
- **Loop** — the shortest way out of a junction and back to it by a different
  track. A junction on a spur has no loop, and the sheet says so.

The result shows the total distance, the estimated walking time, and the
junctions in order with the track between each pair. Tapping a junction closes
the sheet and centres the map on it, leaving follow mode if it was on.

## Where the numbers come from

`content/data/paluma/segments.json` — generated by `paluma_cli` from the track
geometry and junctions — gives one record per junction-to-junction stretch:
distance in metres, walk minutes, bearing and the track's name. The planner is a
shortest-path search over those records, weighted by distance. It never
straight-lines across the scrub.

Every total is an **estimate**. Minutes are the sum of the per-segment
`walk_minutes` at the bundle's flat-terrain pace, and elevation is not included
because the content has no elevation data yet. The sheet repeats the bundle's
estimate label, so the wording cannot drift from `docs/location.md`.

A route is only offered when the segments connect the two junctions. If they do
not, or a junction has no loop, the sheet says that instead of guessing.

## Offline and privacy

The planner reads the offline bundle and computes on the device. It makes no
network request, and it does not record what was planned.

## Not in this slice

- **Drawing the route on the map.** Segments carry junction pairs, not their own
  geometry, so drawing a line between junctions would cut corners the track does
  not. The itinerary focuses junctions instead; a route line needs per-segment
  geometry in the bundle first.
- **GPX export.** Asked about in #20; it needs a route geometry to export.
- **Elevation and climb totals.** Waiting on #14 and a DEM source.
